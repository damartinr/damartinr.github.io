## Visiting the NEUROSEC team at the University of Oxford

My three-month research visit at the University of Oxford has come to an end, and I wanted to take a moment to look back and say thank you.

I spent this time in the Department of Psychiatry, working with the <a href ="https://oxneurosec.com/">Neuroscience, Ethics and Society (NEUROSEC)</a> team and Dr. <a href="https://www.kathrynfrancis.com/">Kathryn Francis</a> on a project about how people perceive and prioritize the moral status of non-human entities, building on my PhD research at the Universidad de Granada under Prof. <a href="https://moralsciencelab.com/people/">Ivar Rodríguez Hannikainen</a> (<a href="https://moralsciencelab.com">Moral Science Lab</a>). I also had the chance to engage with the <a href="https://www.practicalethics.ox.ac.uk/">Uehiro Oxford Institute</a> and the <a ref="https://www.uehiro.ox.ac.uk/the-philosophical-moral-psychology-lab">Philosophical Moral Psychology Lab</a>, and presenting my work there pushed me to rethink and sharpen the direction of my research.

Beyond the research, I got to experience the academic life I had only imagined, formal dinners, walks through the University Parks, punting along the river, or sitting in on talks where people I've long admired presented their work just a few feet away.

Thank you to Kathryn and the whole NEUROSEC team, to <a href="https://www.uehiro.ox.ac.uk/people/dr-joanna-demaree-cotton">Joanna Demaree-Cotton</a> and everyone at the Philosophical Moral Psychology Lab for their generosity with their time. 

These three months gave me far more than I expected, both professionally and personally. Heading back to Granada with new ideas, and collaborations I hope continue well beyond this visit.
